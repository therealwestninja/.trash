## 1. Product Description and Limitations

CERBERUS Companion Web Interface ("the Software") is a browser-based human-machine interface designed to facilitate operator control of and communication with the Unitree Go2 quadruped robot ("the Robot") through the CERBERUS Companion API ("the API").

The Software is provided **as-is** under the MIT License. It is designed to assist trained operators and is not a safety-critical control system certified under IEC 61508 or any equivalent functional safety standard. It must not be relied upon as the sole means of ensuring robot safety.

## 2. Robot Safety Warnings

### 2.1 Physical Hazards

The Unitree Go2 is a motorized robotic device weighing approximately **12 kg** (26 lb). During operation it can:

- Move at speeds up to **3.5 m/s**
- Apply significant joint forces capable of causing **crushing, impact, and entanglement injuries**
- Fall and strike bystanders if terrain, command errors, or software failures cause loss of balance
- Generate heat at motor joints — **do not touch joints during or immediately after operation**

**Before operating the robot:**

- Establish a minimum **1.5-metre (5-foot) clear operating zone** around the robot in all directions
- Ensure all bystanders are aware the robot is active and may move unpredictably
- Keep children and animals outside the operating zone at all times
- Confirm the robot's battery level is sufficient for the intended operation
- Verify the emergency power switch is accessible to the operator at all times
- Never operate the robot on surfaces near stairs, ledges, or water without physical barriers

### 2.2 Software E-Stop Limitations

The Software provides an E-stop function that sends a halt command over the network connection. **This E-stop is software-dependent and network-dependent.** It will NOT function if:

- The network connection between the browser and the CERBERUS API is interrupted
- The browser tab is closed or the page is refreshed
- The backend server crashes or becomes unresponsive
- The robot's onboard software fails to respond to the halt command

**The software E-stop is NOT a substitute for the robot's physical power switch.** The operator must always maintain direct physical access to the robot's power switch as the primary means of stopping the robot in an emergency.

### 2.3 FunScript Playback Safety

The FunScript motion scripting engine allows pre-programmed sequences to be played back directly to the robot's hardware. Before initiating hardware playback of any FunScript:

- Always run the **Script → Validate** function to check for unsafe velocity transitions
- Test the complete sequence in **Simulation mode** before hardware playback
- Ensure the robot is in a clear area with adequate room for all planned movements
- Have an operator physically present with access to the power switch for the entire duration of hardware playback
- Movements generated by FunScript operate outside the robot's autonomous collision-avoidance systems unless the CERBERUS API backend has explicit safety overrides configured

### 2.4 Network Latency and Stale Commands

- Commands sent over WiFi or internet connections may be delayed by **50 ms to several seconds**
- Stale control inputs (e.g., held joystick axis after network drop) may replay when connection is restored
- The interface will indicate stale telemetry after 6 seconds without update — treat a stale indicator as a loss-of-control condition and physically stop the robot
- **Do not operate the robot over high-latency or unreliable network connections**

## 3. Regulatory Compliance Information

### 3.1 Alberta, Canada

**Alberta Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Code, Part 36 — Robots (as amended December 2024)**

The Alberta OHS Code (pursuant to the *Occupational Health and Safety Act*, RSA 2000, c O-2) requires, for any workplace where a robot or robot system is used:

- Written safe work procedures for installation, operation, and use
- Worker training in those safe work procedures
- Compliance with **CAN/CSA-Z434-03 (R2013) — Industrial Robots and Robot Systems: General Safety Requirements**
- Safeguards preventing workers from entering the robot's restricted work envelope during operation

**Note:** Alberta OHS Code Part 36 Section 402(1) exempts "personal robots" and "mobile robots" from some requirements, but employers are strongly advised to apply the general duty provisions of the OHS Act regardless of exemption. CERBERUS users should consult with their workplace health and safety officer.

**Recommended actions for Alberta workplaces:**
- Document a site-specific safe work procedure referencing this software
- Train all operators on emergency stop procedures, operating clearances, and FunScript playback protocols
- Conduct a risk assessment before first deployment
- Contact Alberta Occupational Health and Safety (1-866-415-8690) for guidance on specific deployments

### 3.2 British Columbia, Canada

**WorkSafeBC Occupational Health and Safety Regulation, Part 12 — Tools, Machinery and Equipment**

WorkSafeBC Regulation Section 12.1 general duty provisions and Sections 12.80–12.107 (Machine Safeguarding) apply to workplaces using robotic systems in British Columbia. Key obligations include:

- Guarding of dangerous robot movements to protect workers
- Lockout/tagout procedures for maintenance
- Worker training commensurate with the robot's hazard level

WorkSafeBC guidance on cobots and mobile robots also recommends worker involvement in safety planning. For more information contact WorkSafeBC Prevention at 1-888-621-7233 or visit worksafebc.com.

### 3.3 Federal Canada

**Canada Occupational Health and Safety Regulations (COHSR)** do not expressly address industrial robots. However, the general duty provision under the *Canada Labour Code*, Part II (R.S.C. 1985, c. L-2) Section 124 requires every employer to ensure, so far as reasonably practicable, the health and safety of employees.

**CCOHS (Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety)** guidance on robots and cobots (ccohs.ca) recommends:

- Hazard assessment before deploying any collaborative or mobile robot
- Written safe work practices
- Worker training
- Psychosocial risk management when introducing robots near workers

### 3.4 Other Jurisdictions

This software may be used in jurisdictions with their own robotics or machinery safety regulations. Users outside Alberta and BC are responsible for determining applicable local requirements. The following international standards are widely referenced:

- **ISO 10218-1:2011** — Robots and robotic devices: Safety requirements for industrial robots
- **ISO/TS 15066:2016** — Robots and robotic devices: Collaborative robots
- **IEC 62061** — Safety of machinery: Functional safety of safety-related control systems

## 4. Operator Responsibilities

By using CERBERUS Companion Web Interface to operate a robot, the operator accepts the following responsibilities:

1. **Training:** Ensure all persons operating the Robot through this Software have received adequate training on the Robot's physical characteristics, the Software's capabilities and limitations, and emergency stop procedures.

2. **Risk Assessment:** Conduct a site-specific risk assessment before each deployment, particularly when the operating environment, personnel in the area, or task type has changed.

3. **Physical Safeguards:** Do not rely solely on software-based safety features. Establish physical barriers, exclusion zones, and access control appropriate to the Robot's capabilities.

4. **Supervision:** A trained operator must be physically present and in visual contact with the Robot at all times during remote-control operation. Fully autonomous operations require additional safeguards beyond what this Software provides.

5. **Maintenance:** Ensure the Robot is maintained per the manufacturer's (Unitree) specifications. Do not use this Software with a robot that has known mechanical faults.

6. **Data and Privacy:** Ensure compliance with applicable privacy laws if the Robot's cameras or sensors collect data about identifiable individuals.

7. **Public and Third-Party Risk:** Ensure the Robot is not operated in public spaces without appropriate permissions, barriers, and risk mitigation measures.
